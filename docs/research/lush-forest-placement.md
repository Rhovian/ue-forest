# Lush forest placement: composition, variation and bounded PCG

Research date: 2026-11-07. Intended destination: `docs/research/lush-forest-placement.md`. Research handoff; proposals below have not been tested in the editor.

**Make the forest read as connected plant communities at several scales: a closed crown roof, irregular groups of smaller beech, broad fern/shrub masses, and continuous low green cover connecting roots, wood and soil. Preserve openings between taller masses.** Production artists explicitly recommend clustering with quieter areas, using large trees as anchors and arranging mid-distance vegetation around negative space. This supports changing the spatial relationships before increasing every generator's density. [A1, A2, A3]

**The project already has more palette breadth and colony logic than the earlier research assumed. The next useful experiment is to give those colonies explicit shape, dominant species, dense cores and ragged margins, then vary the beech silhouettes and leaf palette.** This is a proposed extension of `saplings_understory.py:119–143`, `biome_variants.py:32–83` and the sparse-anchor/child-patch method in `meadow.py:103–111`, rather than a diagnosis from current gameplay screenshots. [L2, L3, L4]

**There is no verified universal “lush beech” coverage recipe, typical UE tree-variant count, or safe Nanite foliage instance budget in the reviewed sources.** Numeric brackets below are explicitly artistic trials or calculations. They are not Epic defaults, ecological averages or performance guarantees. The previous report already established the layering/rendering fundamentals; this report extends placement grammar, material data paths and project-specific experiments. [L1:27–42,85–101; E5; A4]

## 2. Clustering and composition: why individual plants look planted

Danny Weinbaum describes deliberately dense groups and comparatively empty areas, and warns against evenly distributing foliage. Simon Barle describes large anchor trees with smaller growth and life stages around them, rotated unique trunks and avoiding alignments. Tyler Smith builds depth by leaving negative space through mid-range tree/bush groups. These are complementary composition practices, not prescriptions that every trunk should acquire an identical ring. [A1, A2, A3]

**Design inference for this forest:** “one-off” placement can come from a similar empty moat around every plant, the same spacing everywhere, disconnected plant/material patches, or every species independently filling the whole scene. Conversely, feeding every species exactly the same colony field can make each colony an identical mixed bouquet. Neither failure is verified in the current level; the existing shared field makes the latter a specific hypothesis to inspect. [A1, A2; L2:119–123]

Proposed placement grammar, using the installed palette:

- **Habitat:** choose damp bank, closed interior, light gap or exceptional meadow stretch first. Habitat drives which colony forms are eligible; it does not mandate maximum density in every layer. The coast retains the operator's abrupt edge, with meadow only in selected stretches. [Design proposal; L0:19–21,236–254; N1]
- **Colony:** use an elongated fern drift, a compact shrub mass or low herb pocket as a spatial unit. Pick a dominant plant family per colony, with a smaller companion share; vary radius/aspect and merge neighbouring edges into larger irregular masses. For a first trial, compare 80:20 and 60:40 dominant/companion selections rather than calling either natural law. [Design proposal; A1, A2; L3]
- **Individual:** scatter varied whole plants inside the colony. Let silhouettes overlap within dense cores while preserving trunk clearance and the footpath. Add a few edge outliers rather than uniformly thinning the entire group. [Design proposal; A1; L8:39–45; L0:53]
- **Composition:** retain low moss openings between tall masses, sightline windows through successive trunk planes and a small number of prominent log/rock/plant assemblies. Negative space need not expose bare dirt: low green ground can remain continuous under a gap in shrubs. [Design proposal; A3; L0:204,217–219]

### Concrete UE 5.8 mechanisms

Epic documents Spatial Noise writing an attribute, Distance for spatial relationships, density remapping/curve remapping, randomized Self Pruning, Copy Points and Projection. Self Pruning removes point-bound intersections; it is not automatically a Poisson-disc botanical simulation. A minimum-distance distribution prevents collisions but does not by itself create colonies. [E1]

**Proposed graph structure:** sparse eligible anchors → assign `ColonyID`, colony form and dominant palette → copy bounded child patterns → perturb/stretch/rotate each pattern → project to ground → apply local core/edge retention → select weighted variants → retain path/water/trunk keepouts. This extends the existing meadow/children implementation; do not introduce an actor for every clump. [Design proposal; L4:103–111; L9:172–209]

For an illustrative retention field use `p = clamp(core_falloff × habitat_suitability × edge_breakup, 0, 1)` and keep points where a stable per-point random value is below `p`. Do not confuse a Density Filter, which thresholds values, with random acceptance proportional to the value. The formula is proposed logic, not a claim about the current template. Start with broad colony shapes of roughly 3–8 m and edge breakup around 0.5–1.5 m; these are trial feature sizes. First compare shape at the same final plant count. [Design proposal; L2:63–65; E1]

For banks, use distance to the bank/shoreline feature, not a constant increase over an entire 30 m band. For gaps, derive sapling eligibility from explicit gap inputs; do not assume a generator named `Gaps` actually samples canopy openings—the current sapling branch receives the plain shore filter. For ground-contact assemblies, distance to a trunk/wood footprint is useful for skirts, but crown-sized bounds must not erase understory. [Design proposal; L2:115–134; L9:82–91,146–149; E2]

Self-prune anchor centres or trunk footprints where clearance is needed. Do not self-prune all frond/crown bounds to disjoint boxes: that would defeat visual overlap. Preserve this project's unique `AssetType` per group, low child bounds, ground projection and distinct child seeds. Shared meshes must be counted by mesh, not only by reported group. [Design proposal; L8:30–49]

## 3. Layering and coverage: local targets rather than a uniform wall

Beech ecology supports **spatially differentiated** lushness. JNCC describes understorey communities varying with soil and slope in rich-soil beech woodland. Feldmann et al. studied gap regeneration in a Slovakian virgin beech forest and report different regeneration dynamics by gap position/size; small gaps can close by lateral crown growth. This supports a gap/interior/bank vocabulary, not the inference that mature beech always has equally dense shrubs beneath every crown. [N1, N2]

The operator intentionally requests a heightened mossy summer forest, so botanical evidence informs the arrangement without overriding its concept targets. For example, RHS identifies wood anemone as a spring-flowering, spreading woodland plant; Kew describes bluebell displays in April/May. Summer flowers in these references are an artistic seasonal combination already requested, not a reason to silently remove their accents. [N3, N4; L0:15,55,202]

The table gives **proposed trial coverage**, extending rather than replacing the older report's initial brackets. Ground percentages mean the union of plant footprints in selected occupied patches, not count density or percentage of the whole frame; layers overlap and must not sum to 100%. Canopy is instead the fixed upward-view sky fraction. Percentages are not established production norms. [Design proposal; L1:27–42; L0:204–224]

| Layer | Trial for this art direction | Where restraint matters |
|---|---|---|
| Mature canopy | Use the existing approximately 15%-sky target, checked in the prescribed view. | Do not maximize every view's enclosure; preserve the clearing opening and framed shore view. [L0:209,214,264] |
| Sub-canopy/small beech | Compare leafy screens occupying roughly 10–20% of selected interior-patch plan area, rising to 30–50% in a gap regeneration pocket. | Keep mature stems visible between screens. Use age/crown forms rather than only scaled mature trees. [Design proposal; N2; A2] |
| Shrub | Compare roughly 10–25% patch coverage, concentrated into a few broad masses. | Interior sightline windows and open ground around the clearing mass; species authority remains a lead decision. [Design proposal; L0:217–219; N1] |
| Fern | Compare 40–70% footprint union **inside** a fern colony, then vary colony occupancy independently. | Raised foliage can thin between colonies while low moss remains continuous. [Design proposal; A1; L0:202–204] |
| Herb/flowers | Dense low leaf clumps in pockets, but flowers only accenting selected margins/banks; do not carpet the interior with blossoms. | Check the visible accent proportion rather than multiplying herb candidates across the island. [Design proposal; L0:202,213,224] |
| Ground/moss/litter | In the near interior, aim first for the written “almost no bare leaf litter” target using the existing floor/coating, then add relief only where it changes the silhouette. | Avoid raising every pixel into another overlapping mesh layer. [Design proposal; L0:204; L6; E6] |

Within-colony density depends on **actual footprint**. A useful bookkeeping calculation is `nominal area = count × mean footprint`; this overestimates union area when plants overlap. Use top-down/oblique inspection to establish union coverage rather than treating 0.6 fern candidates/m² as 60% coverage. Existing 6,000/ha and 20,000/ha are generator input candidates, not verified survivors or observed cover. [Calculation definition; L2:7,82–86]

Ground fill should have three scales: continuous material colour/normal variation; selected low leaf/litter/ivy clump relief; larger ferns and roots at silhouettes/contacts. Smith's breakdown separates forest-floor foliage clumps from the tiling texture and treats repeated cutout shapes carefully. The existing floor macro textures and moss coating supply the continuous base; projected decals can add local edge/litter variation, but cannot supply fern height or moss mound silhouette. [A3; L6; L7; E5]

**Asset limit:** no verified free cushion-moss listing is available in the project direction; Nordic Forest Ledge Moss was rejected because it read as pale stone plates. Use existing material continuity and suitable installed mossy rocks as local forms; do not resurrect the rejected asset or claim a new moss geometry import. Lichen is an optional restrained bark/stone tint/mask idea, not a required new species/asset or proof of dampness. Keep it subordinate to the requested moss; no project-specific lichen texture or material was verified. [L0:169–175; design proposal]

## 4. Variety from a small beech palette

Rudy Lamotte reports making two or three variations per tree type depending on visibility/density; another documented forest/archviz scene uses five forest trees plus a foreground tree. These are **examples**, not a typical AAA requirement. Weinbaum recommends testing a plant in context before producing more variants; Ilari Lampinen stresses different ages/shapes and cautions against excessive scaling. [A4, A5, A1, A6]

**Proposed palette audit:** inspect all mature Beech A–D from the fixed walker view and rotated angles, and classify crown width, crown-start height, trunk fork and root form. Keep any distinct forms; replace redundant silhouettes with a small number of beech PVE exports if necessary. A working trial could retain four mature forms plus the existing three saplings, adding only a missing mid-age/small-crown beech role. This is a testable starting palette, not evidence that four variants suffice at every distance. Existing source comments describe A–D as upright forest trees and offer PVE exports for shore-root variation. [Design proposal; L0:171–173; L3:32–36; A4]

Suggested variation envelope, all **proposals**:

| Parameter | First controlled comparison | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Yaw | Stable random 0–360°, with a few hero trunks deliberately faced. | Distinct trunk sides visible; avoid aligning forks. Existing helper already sets this yaw range. [L9:137–143; A2] |
| Scale | Around each chosen age/shape's intended size, try 0.9–1.1 before widening. Retain the present canopy scale for the initial placement A/B. | Crown closure, root contact and plausible leaf/trunk proportions; do not interpret this as an instruction to shrink the current forest. [L5; A6] |
| Lean | Trial mature beech pitch/roll within approximately ±2°, saplings ±5°; keep fern bases properly seated. | Grounded roots and upright woodland character. Verify whether `OrientUpward` cancels the intended tilt. [L9:165–167; L2:140–143] |
| Tint | Start with two restrained neighbouring green endpoints; separately compare approximately ±5% and ±10% brightness around the baseline. | Variety visible across several crowns without yellow autumn trees or alternating candy colours. These are trial multipliers, not measured leaf reflectance. [Design proposal; L0:15,55,202; A7] |
| Colony correlation | Give a colony a base tint/scale role and small independent per-plant perturbation. | Coherent green drifts without identical sibling transforms or same-shaped bouquets. [Design proposal; L8:45; A7] |

Mix secondary **understory silhouettes** already installed—Lady Fern, ivy and shrubs—according to habitat. Do not add a second canopy species: current art direction explicitly excludes conifers/birch and says beech-only. Hazel is present in `SHRUB_VARIANTS` but the direction still lists its second-species ruling as open; installed paths alone do not settle that authority conflict. Lead should check the issue ruling before expanding hazel use; the installed Huckleberry Oak or grouped beech saplings are available visual fallbacks. [L3:38–54; L0:49,173,183,219]

### Getting colour into the material without one material instance per plant

1. **Independent random tint:** `PerInstanceRandom` supplies a stable different value per instanced static mesh. Map it through a narrow, artist-selected green ramp in the shared leaf material; it is not a complete habitat/age control. Test the actual skinned beech path separately rather than assuming a static-ISM example proves it. [E3; L9:156–161]
2. **Spatial tint:** use world-space low-frequency noise or a shared sampled colour field for coherent patches; overlay a smaller per-instance variation. David Holland's Meadows breakdown uses a large-scale variation texture with subtle smooth colour transitions. This is a transferable palette technique, not a request for that scene's stylized shading. [A7]
3. **Authored PCG tint:** carry named attributes, e.g. `LeafTint` and `Health`, through placement, pack them at the mesh spawner and read the resulting `PerInstanceCustomData` slots in the material. Installed UE 5.8 has static and skinned **ByAttribute** packers and `AttributeSelectors`; the deprecated `AttributeNames` interface should not be copied from old tutorials. The packers' existence does **not** verify that the project's Biome Core spawner graphs already expose/preserve those attributes. Lead must inspect the graph and prove two different instances receive different values. [U1, U2]
4. **Custom Primitive Data:** CPD is per scene primitive/component, rather than automatically per member of a multi-instance component. Use it for a component-wide palette control, not as a substitute for per-instance packed data. Epic documents indexed scalar/vector parameters and its batching advantage over separate dynamic material instances. [E4; U1]

Seasonal/health controls can distinguish lighter new-growth green, mature green and a little desaturated damaged foliage while keeping summer. They should be named, bounded controls and masked to leaves/frond tips, not unrestricted hue randomization or entire-plant RGB multiplication. Preserve bark grey and the subsurface response. Exact tint parameters on the installed masters were not verified; source paths and material usage lists are not an inspection of their live graphs. [Design proposal; L0:55,203; L3:86–96; E3]

## 5. Colour and grading for the enchanted read

The palette authority is `interior_01`: cool green dominance, blue/violet accents and neutral grey bark. The concepts carry much of their colour through moss, fern masses and lit translucent leaves, with smaller white/blue flowers. Do not borrow the exaggerated pale bark or turn every leaf into a separate saturated colour. [L0:202–204; V1]

Smith keeps texture value variation subtle, establishes local asset colours and then finishes with saturation/LUT work. A separate UE5 forest breakdown describes lowering overall saturation to control warm lighting. Thus “more colour” can mean stronger relationships and selective accents rather than higher global saturation. [A3, A8]

**Proposed palette test:** retain dark cool-green ivy/shrub masses, mid-green beech crowns, somewhat warmer/lighter fern tips and moss, small white and blue/violet accents, and restrained brown litter glimpses. Compare the existing grade with a modest mids-only saturation increase (for example 1.05 then 1.10) at locked exposure/time. These values are artistic test increments, not industry targets or engine defaults. Inspect midtone separation and backlit leaves before altering more materials. [Design proposal; L0:15,202; E7]

There is no source-backed numeric HSV saturation or fixed value range for this enchanted forest. The usable target is relative: dark readable foreground, separated trunks/green masses, bright but detailed sunlit foliage and small accents. Epic recommends keeping film response consistent project-wide and making artistic adjustments through colour grading; its tonemapper can drive sufficiently bright colour toward white. The shore view specifically asks for readable shade and bright sea/sky. [E7; L0:261–264]

Moss is the strongest installed continuous colour carrier: tune its floor, root and wood response together, under the same lighting, while retaining roughness variation. The coating's `MossBrightness` and floor `MacroBrightness` are actual script controls, but script defaults do not establish current MI overrides. Lichen could add a restrained desaturated grey/olive counter-colour on selected surfaces; no lichen-specific lushness rule or verified local implementation was found. [L6:47–57; L7:20–47; proposed lichen use]

Flowers require the correct leaf/flower silhouettes. The installed herb list is white wood anemone; a verified free blue/violet flower listing was not established in this pass. Keep the documented free-Fab acquisition workflow and use the installed white accents while that gap is resolved; do not blue-tint every white anemone and call it a bluebell. Mist/wisps are separate systems with their own direction, not substitutes for missing plant masses. [L3:79–83; L0:158–175; AGENTS.md:17]

## 6. Density with bounded generation and rendering costs

**Separate four budgets:** sampler candidates, retained instances, resident/visible geometry and animation/shadow/material work. Epic cautions that practical limits depend on content/hardware; aggregate foliage can defeat ordinary Nanite simplification/occlusion. Nanite's documented 16-million streamed-in instance ceiling includes non-Nanite instances, and is a hard limit, not a viable foliage target. [E5, E6]

Nanite Foliage's assemblies, voxels and skinning address different parts of representation. It remains experimental in current 5.8 documentation. Its detailed example tree has 12 unique assembly parts, 2,160 part instances and 850 bones: these are **within one tree**, not a count of affordable whole forest trees. Do not mix assembly-part counts, PCG points and plant instances in the same budget. [E8]

### Generation calculation for this checkout

`candidate_estimate` computes `min(requested_per_ha, 10000/(2*extent_cm/100)^2) × bounding_area_ha`. Reading `forest_foot.json["island_m"]` printed `[-890, 992, -1258, 1254]`, giving **472.7584 ha**. Therefore an uncapped 20,000/ha layer asks for about **9.46 million** candidates; 120,000/ha asks for **56.73 million**; 600,000/ha would be **283.66 million** before the cell cap. All are calculations, not new generation runs. [L9:100–121; C2]

The local lesson records approximately 180–185 million in the earlier 600,000/ha incident; the above uncapped calculation is larger. Do not reinterpret the old observation as today's formula result: earlier bounds/cell extents and rounding were not reconstructed. The installed guard caps each generator estimate at 60 million, but several generators and their intermediate arrays can coexist; it is not a whole-editor memory guarantee. Keep that guard and the existing memory cancellation, and estimate the sum of branches as well as the largest one. [L8:11–28; L9:100–121; design implication]

For a **proposed bounded experiment**, 100 accepted anchors × 40 child plants gives about 4,000 child instances; 1,000 anchors gives about 40,000. This excludes visible parent plants and variant sharing. The anchors still have island-wide candidate cost in the current generator, but children are created only at retained anchors. Increase patch occupancy/shape separately from local child density, and check the actual output at each step because the template's noise response is nonlinear. [Calculation; L4:37–39,103–111; L8:16–28]
