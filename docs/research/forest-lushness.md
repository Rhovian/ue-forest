# Forest lushness: layers, coverage and rendering costs

Research date: 2026-10-22. Scope: issue #9's summer, old-growth **beech** forest with an Olympic temperate-rainforest feel, in UE 5.8. Research only; no project decisions or editor-tested prescription.

**The missing read is chiefly vegetation between the trunks and the textured floor, continuous overhead foliage, and growth covering woody surfaces—not a shortage of isolated rocks and stumps.** The supplied coastal screenshots show broad exposed floor and almost no raised ground plants; step 8b and the shared moss coating are still pending. This is a visual diagnosis of those views, not a measurement of inland canopy closure. [L1, L2, L4]

**Epic's transferable pattern is layered, spatially related composition:** large/small trees, clustered plants, surface-fitting ground detail, and assemblies with subordinate growth. Electric Dreams demonstrates this composition; Biome Core provides reusable placement and runtime-detail mechanisms; Nanite Foliage addresses the geometry/animation representation. None of the reviewed Epic sources supplies a universal stems-per-hectare or plants-per-square-metre recipe. [E1–E5]

**Lushness and frame budget are separate measurements.** Dense cover costs depend on visible coverage, depth overlap, materials, shadow updates and lighting representation, across platforms. The M2 Max is a secondary testing constraint, not the final performance specification. The existing interior capture already exceeds 33.3 ms before understory; removing a future understory alone cannot fix that measured baseline. [E6–E9; L2:67–82; calculation: 1000/30]

## 1. What the target looks like, and what is missing

Olympic's own description identifies a layered canopy, epiphytes, large old trees, nurse logs and slowly decaying wood supporting moss, seedlings and fungi. It also explicitly notes that elk can keep the understory open. Thus “lush” need not mean an impassable shrub wall: overhead enclosure, low green coverage and colonized trunks/logs can coexist with sightlines. Its principal trees are conifers; translating this to the issue's beech-only palette is an artistic hybrid, not botanical reconstruction. [N1; I0]

### Layers and useful density/coverage measures

The following numbers are **proposed visual trial brackets**, not documented Epic defaults, measured ecological norms or performance promises. The reviewed material supports layers and patchiness, but does not justify calling one numerical recipe “typical.” Each percentage is projected ground coverage *within a selected forest patch*, except canopy closure; layers may overlap and must not be summed. Counts mean whole plants/clumps, not leaves or triangles. The operator owns any adoption. [Design inference from N1, E1–E5]

| Layer | Contribution to lushness | Trial bracket / useful measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Mature canopy | Roof, sky occlusion, dappled shade, large scale | Compare the existing mean against the issue's ~150/ha; inspect 70–90% overhead foliage coverage in an enclosed reference patch, while retaining deliberate gaps. **Proposed coverage target**, not measured crown closure. [I0; N1] |
| Sub-canopy | Fills the vertical gap below mature crowns; interrupts distant trunk rows | Trial 30–100 smaller beech stems/ha **inside selected occupied patches**, not blanket coverage. Distinguish these from the mature-tree count. [Design inference; E1] |
| Saplings | Regeneration, irregular screens and lower leafy silhouettes | Trial 100–500/ha within regeneration patches occupying 10–30% of eligible ground; sparse elsewhere. For example, 20% occupied gives 20–100/ha averaged over the whole eligible area. [Design inference; I0, E4] |
| Shrubs | Broad middle-height leaf masses, especially banks/gaps | Trial 10–30% patch coverage, leaving open pockets and path sightlines. Use the existing Huckleberry Oak only as the agreed silhouette stand-in. [Design inference; I0, N1] |
| Ferns | Large, legible radial fronds and intermediate-height depth | Trial 20–50% patch coverage, or ~0.5–2 whole plants/m² **inside fern colonies**, adjusted to actual plant footprint. A USFS synthesis reports ~25% swordfern cover for one western Cascades hemlock/swordfern association; that is a contextual example, not a universal Olympic/beech density. [Design inference; N2] |
| Low herbs/ground cover | Overlapping leaf silhouettes and ground contact | Trial 40–70% local coverage; ~2–8 small clumps/m² in occupied pockets is a starting test only. Keep beech litter visible; use grass primarily where it suits the chosen reference, rather than treating a lawn as rainforest. [Design inference; N1, E2] |
| Moss | Broad green continuity across floor, roots and deadwood | Material coverage first; optional relief tufts at close contact/silhouette. No verified universal moss percentage; judge visible coating continuity against a selected reference. Existing coating research supplies implementation detail. [N1; L7:5–7,174–184] |
| Debris and deadwood | Fallen branches, decay, microtopography; nurse-log growth connects vertical layers | Use branches as local scatter and whole trunks as larger assemblies, with a range of decay forms. Judge footprint, thickness and colonization, not only “logs/ha.” Current log is a shallow piece; nurse-log child is currently one mushroom cluster. [N1; L3:48–50,68–70] |

**Why canopy counts alone are insufficient:** for a deliberately simplified random-disc model, covered fraction is `1-exp(-N*pi*(D/2)^2/10000)`. At 70 crowns/ha, diameters 8/12/16 m give approximately 30/55/76% cover; at 150/ha, a 12 m diameter gives ~82%. This is an illustrative calculation, **not a simulation of Beech A–D**: crown holes, clustering, height and overlap violate its assumptions. Measure actual overhead coverage before inferring enclosure from stem count. [Calculation from stated assumptions; tree targets I0/L1]

| **Nanite Foliage / PVE export** | Geometry representation and wind for the selected meshes, combined with the placement systems above | A rendering/animation workflow, not an additional landscape spawner. **Runtime GPU limitations matter to layer selection:** procedurally instanced GPU meshes are not persisted and currently lack HLOD, collision/navigation, ray tracing and Affect Distance Field Lighting support. They suit optional visual microdetail; they cannot provide the complete off-screen distance-field occlusion for a structural canopy. GPU processing also carries upload/setup costs, so group GPU work and avoid repeated CPU/GPU transfers. [E15; final suitability statement is inference]

Grass density is **not** the project's `PointsPerSquareMeter`: installed `LandscapeGrass.cpp:2070` computes count from area in cm² multiplied by density, divided by `1000×1000`, i.e. per **100 m²** before scaling/rounding. Its header's “per 10 square meters” wording is ambiguous against this implementation. Do not copy numeric density values between the systems. Cull distances and quality-dependent density controls exist, but verify actual Nanite fade/cull behaviour on the chosen path. [L10; L8:101–104]

For a 1 km² World Partition level, partition persistent generation so output streams in cells; use runtime detail only if its optional scope is adopted. PCG supports Data Layer/HLOD assignments, but this does not override the GPU instance limitations above. A dense local patch can therefore be studied without populating the whole island at its finest density. [E5, E16; I0; final sentence is design inference]

| Factor | Verified renderer behaviour | Proposed diagnostic / art implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Enclosure and shade** | Lumen supplies indirect colour bounce and sky shadowing; foliage shading can transmit backface light using Subsurface Color. [E17] | Compare inland canopy-up and walker views to the coast. Preserve deep readable shade with selected lit leaves; globally darkening an open floor will not create overhead enclosure. [Inference; L4, N1] |
| **Software-Lumen representation** | Distance-field tracing lists static/instanced meshes and Landscape, excludes WPO deformation, and requires foliage distance-field participation. Separately, UE 5.8 supports limited skeletal Surface Cache coverage. [E18] | The latter is **not** proof of full skinned distance-field tracing. Inspect Lumen Scene/Surface Cache and temporarily compare screen traces off/on before diagnosing a washed-out floor solely as too few trees. No representation bug is established here. [Diagnostic inference] |
| **Exposure** | Histogram/Basic metering adapt luminance toward middle grey; Manual exposure fixes it. [E19] | Lock exposure during density/lighting A/B comparisons, then test adaptation on a beach-to-interior walk. An automatic lift of shaded ground can undermine the intended shade hierarchy. The screenshots alone cannot identify exposure settings. [Inference; L4] |
| **Fog** | Volumetric extinction and directional scattering control depth/shafts. Epic suggests low global density with stronger directional contribution or localized denser areas to retain shafts without globally heavy fog. [E20] | Put mist where the design asks for it—hollows and layered distance. Excessive near-camera haze can wash out local foliage contrast. Fog cannot replace absent close fronds. [Art inference; I0, L4] |
| **Colour/material response** | Grading affects saturation/contrast and tonal ranges; exposure and filmic tonemapping alter perceived colour. [E21] | Compare a neutral grade first. Current pale colour has no verified single cause. [Art inference; L4] |

Specific code-reading correction: `DryBrightening` multiplies **sand's** base colour before the floor blend; it is not a global leaf/moss brightness control. The issue records a tuned value of 0.45, but changing it cannot directly darken the nonsand floor. Low/flat/noise masks control moss placement; current MI values still require lead-side inspection. [L5:93–95,119–145; L2:112]

## 5. Performance: general costs first

### Platform-independent cost picture

- **Visible depth and material work:** leaf cards, holes and stacked foliage create overdraw; dense aggregate geometry also resists ordinary Nanite simplification. Epic recommends evaluating opaque geometric foliage instead of assuming Nanite card conversion is faster. Voxel foliage targets distant aggregate representation, not all near-camera material or shadow costs. Profile Nanite Overdraw and material/raster work in the dense walker view. [E6, E8; profiling application is inference]
- **VSM updates:** skinned deformation and WPO/PDO invalidate cached shadow pages; moving the sun also invalidates its cache. Tight bounds and animation cutoffs help. Epic explicitly allows contact shadows as an alternative for some grass/foliage, with quality tradeoffs. Retain structural canopy shadows; test selective small-cover casting. Increasing directional resolution bias lowers shadow resolution/cost. Do not mark genuinely animated geometry Static just to suppress invalidation. [E7; last two selection statements are proposed use]
- **Animation and shadow cost are different:** inexpensive bone updates do not imply inexpensive forest shadows. Epic recommends Animation Min Screen Size for distant skinned foliage, with default 0.1 via `r.Skinning.DefaultAnimationMinScreenSize`; disabled animation uses bind pose. This also changes visible motion and belongs to the operator's deferred wind/optimization choices. [E8; I0; L2:79–82]
- **Lumen participation:** Global Distance Field tracing reduces sensitivity to instance overlap, but distance-field updates still depend on instance count. Epic recommends removing insignificant contributors where appropriate. Foliage reflection tracing can also be reduced using `r.Lumen.Reflections.MaxRoughnessToTraceForFoliage`; verify the material uses a qualifying shading model. Retain important enclosure/occlusion contributors when testing these levers. [E9; last sentence is inference]
- **Generation/streaming:** high island-wide counts affect memory and generation even when not all are visible. Hierarchical runtime detail limits fine scatter to nearby sources; it does not make those meshes free to render. A radius increase from 32 m to 64 m covers four times the area before masking, an elementary scaling reason to measure radius separately from density. [E12, E15; count/radius consequence is inference/calculation]
- **Lighting/atmosphere/resolution:** Epic's High/Epic Lumen budgets target 60/30 fps on consoles, not all hardware. Fog resolution and volume overdraw are separate GPU costs. Resolution/scalability can be comparison levers, but the issue currently asks for native PIE testing; an upscaled demo result is not that acceptance result. [E9, E20; I0]

**Proposed optimization order for a lush result:** measure cost by layer; preserve canopy enclosure and visible fern/shrub masses; reduce distant tiny cover, its animation, its shadow casting, unnecessary lighting participation and shader complexity before simply deleting every second visible plant. Cluster coverage can remain legible while detail radius changes. This is a visual prioritization proposal; the issue's “understory tuned down first” policy remains the operator's decision. [Inference from E6–E9, E12, E15; policy I0]

### Secondary concern: usable testing on this Mac

Epic's current macOS table lists software Lumen on M1+, and Nanite/VSM on M2+ as Beta. This is feature support, not a dense-cover performance forecast. The table does not separately certify every experimental Nanite Foliage or GPU PCG combination. [E22]

The project's own trees-only route measured 23.5 ms at the beach and 45.9 ms in the interior, with interior ShadowDepths ~22 ms, Nanite VisBuffer ~4.5 ms and empty-island GPU cost ~16 ms. These are reported historical measurements under a frozen 10:00 sun, Epic, 1080p PIE/software Lumen; no new capture was run. They identify current local shadow headroom, not a general “Apple foliage cost” or final target budget. [L2:65–82]

A useful lead-side comparison would hold camera, resolution, exposure, sun and wind fixed, and record the same views with: structural plants; low cover; low-cover shadows; motion; atmosphere. Then repeat with the moving sun and streaming walk. Report both image coverage and incremental ms, including p95. These are proposed evidence requests, not checks performed by this worker. [Method inference from E7, E12, E19; existing harness L2:65]
